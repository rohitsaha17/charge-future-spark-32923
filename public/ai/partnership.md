## Who can become an A Plus Charge partner?

Any of the following:

- Commercial property owners (malls, hotels, fuel stations, parking complexes)
- Residential complex RWAs and apartment developers
- Fleet operators (cab aggregators, logistics, e-commerce last-mile)
- State / city authorities and PSUs (existing partnerships with GMDA, AAI)
- Highway plaza operators along NH-27, NH-37, NH-44

## What are the two partnership models?

**CAPEX model** — the partner buys the hardware. A Plus Charge handles everything else (installation, DISCOM approvals, software, payments, customer service, uptime monitoring). Partner takes the majority revenue share. Best for property owners with capital who want long-term yield from a high-traffic site. Projected ROI 15–20% over five years for qualifying sites.

**OPEX model** — A Plus Charge funds the hardware. Partner contributes space, grid connection, and security. A Plus Charge takes a larger share of revenue to recoup the capex over the contract term (5–7 years typical). Best for partners with prime location but no capex appetite.

## What does "projected 15–20% ROI" mean?

It is a forward-looking estimate for high-traffic sites under the CAPEX model. The number is derived from a representative site doing ~60 units/day at the tariff that location supports, net of network and maintenance costs, over a five-year window. Actual returns depend on local traffic, tariff, downtime, and DISCOM cost. The interactive ROI calculator at https://www.apluscharge.com/partner lets a prospective partner plug in their own assumptions.

## How long does it take to go live?

Typical timeline from signed agreement to commissioned station:

| Step | Duration |
|------|---------:|
| Site feasibility survey | 1 week |
| DISCOM / municipal approval | 4–8 weeks |
| Civil works + hardware install | 1–2 weeks |
| Commissioning + app onboarding | 1 week |
| **Total** | **6–12 weeks** |

DISCOM approval is the slowest step in most cases. A Plus Charge handles the paperwork; partner support is required only for site documents (NOC, electricity board approval if not already in place).

## What's the minimum site requirement?

- Clear footprint: 2 car-bays for a 60 kW DC station, smaller for AC
- Power: 415 V three-phase supply (or scope for transformer upgrade)
- Visibility: signage approval (mall, plaza, highway)
- Accessibility: 24×7 access for charging customers (or agreed daytime hours)

## Does the partner do anything operationally?

Under both models, A Plus Charge runs operations end-to-end:

- 24×7 remote uptime monitoring
- Customer support (in-app + phone)
- Payment processing
- Hardware maintenance and breakdown service
- Software updates and roaming integrations

The partner's role is to provide the space, ensure access, and (in the CAPEX model) collect the revenue share.

## Is there a co-branding option?

Yes. The station is co-branded with the partner's logo and naming (e.g. "A Plus Charge × [Partner Name]"). On the app and on physical signage.

## Can I cancel?

Both models have a defined contract term and exit clauses documented in the Partner Agreement. The OPEX model has a longer minimum term because A Plus Charge funds the upfront capex.

## What's the difference between A Plus Charge and a national operator?

A Plus Charge is a regional specialist for Northeast India. National operators (Tata Power EZ Charge, Statiq, ChargeZone, Magenta) have very limited presence in the Northeast — A Plus Charge built the first regional network. Differentiators:

- Local DISCOM relationships and approval expertise
- Engineering adapted to monsoon, humidity, and voltage instability
- Exclusive partnerships with state agencies (GMDA, AAI)
- Customer service in Assamese / Hindi / English
- Faster site activation in the region vs national operators
